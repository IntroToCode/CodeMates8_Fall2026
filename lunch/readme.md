# Kung Pao Chicken (宫保鸡丁)

## Description

A Sichuan classic named after Ding Baozhen, a Qing dynasty governor of Sichuan whose official title was Gongbao. Tender diced chicken and crunchy peanuts are tossed in a glossy sauce that is sweet, sour, savory, and lightly numbing from Sichuan peppercorns.

### Ingredients:

**Chicken and marinade**
- [ ] 400 g boneless chicken thighs, diced into 1.5 cm cubes
- [ ] 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- [ ] 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine
- [ ] 2 tsp cornstarch

**Sauce**
- [ ] 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- [ ] 1 tsp dark soy sauce
- [ ] 1.5 tbsp Chinkiang black vinegar
- [ ] 1.5 tbsp sugar
- [ ] 1 tsp cornstarch
- [ ] 3 tbsp water

**Stir fry**
- [ ] 2 tbsp neutral oil
- [ ] 8 dried red chilies, snipped in half and seeds shaken out
- [ ] 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns
- [ ] 3 garlic cloves, sliced
- [ ] 1 tbsp ginger, minced
- [ ] 4 scallions, white parts cut into 2 cm pieces
- [ ] 60 g roasted unsalted peanuts

**To serve**
- [ ] Steamed white rice

### Steps for preparation:

1. Marinate the chicken: toss the diced chicken with 1 tbsp light soy sauce, the Shaoxing wine, and 2 tsp cornstarch. Let sit 10 minutes while you prep the rest.
2. Mix the sauce: in a small bowl, stir together the light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, black vinegar, sugar, 1 tsp cornstarch, and water until the sugar dissolves.
3. Bloom the aromatics: heat the oil in a wok over medium low heat. Add the dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns and stir for 30 to 45 seconds, until fragrant and the chilies darken slightly. Don't let them turn black or the dish will taste bitter.
4. Stir fry the chicken: turn the heat to high, add the chicken, and spread it in a single layer. Stir fry about 3 minutes, until the outside is no longer pink.
5. Add the garlic, ginger, and scallion whites. Stir fry about 1 minute, until fragrant.
6. Finish: give the sauce a quick stir (the cornstarch settles), pour it into the wok, and toss until the sauce thickens and turns glossy, about 1 minute. Make sure the chicken is cooked through.
7. Turn off the heat, stir in the peanuts, and serve immediately over steamed rice.

### Notes:

- Have everything chopped and the sauce mixed before you turn on the heat; the cooking goes fast.
- Add the peanuts at the very end so they stay crunchy.
- Thighs stay juicier than breast. If using breast, don't skip the marinade.
- For less heat, use fewer chilies and leave them whole instead of snipping them.
- To stretch it with vegetables, add diced celery, bell pepper, or zucchini along with the garlic.
